About Ekta NexCus

Built by people who've done the work, for people doing the work.

Ekta NexCus began as a conversation about time — who has enough of it, who doesn't, and why that gap keeps widening. It has since grown into a policy initiative and a set of practical platforms.

Our story

From one district's problem to a national proposal

Ekta NexCus was founded by Ekta, whose background spans social media analysis, CSR programme management — including work with organisations like Samsung India and VisionSpring — and years of grassroots community work across Maharashtra. That work kept surfacing the same pattern: long, inflexible working hours quietly eroding family life, health and community participation, especially for workers in the unorganised sector.

Mahesh Vanjire, based in Pune, joined as co-builder, bringing the platform-building and on-the-ground organising that turned an idea into Suvidha and the broader Ekta NexCus initiative.

Together, the two strands of this work — policy advocacy and practical worker platforms — are meant to reinforce each other: real service to real workers today, in service of a bigger case for reform tomorrow.

Where we work

The Kolhapur–Pune corridor

We focus on Kolhapur, Sangli, Satara, Pune and Solapur — a corridor with a large unorganised-sector workforce and strong community networks we can build with directly. All worker-facing material is offered in Marathi, Hindi and English.

What guides us

Principles, not slogans

Dignity first

Every proposal we make treats work-life balance as a matter of dignity, not a perk.

Honesty in claims

We don't publish numbers we can't stand behind, and we say clearly when something is a proposal, not policy.

Community-led

Programmes are shaped with the corridor's own workers, not designed at a distance and handed down.

Practical first

Suvidha exists to solve real, immediate problems — the policy case is built from that ground truth.
